Reading List

Books and papers that are actually worth your time. Curated for advisors — not for MBAs. Annotated with why it matters for practice.

The Psychology of Money
Morgan Housel
The best single book for understanding why clients behave the way they do with money. Every page has a practical application for client conversations. Essential reading before any behavioural finance module.
Against the Gods: The Remarkable Story of Risk
Peter Bernstein
The intellectual history of how humans learned to quantify and manage risk. Gives advisors a language for explaining uncertainty to clients that goes beyond standard deviation.
The Little Book of Common Sense Investing
John Bogle
Worth reading for the client-communication skills alone — Bogle explains compounding better than anyone. Know the argument cold before you recommend any actively managed fund.

Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms that come up most often in advisor practice. Written for advisors, not academics.

Provision for Credit Losses
An amount a bank sets aside to cover loans it expects won't be repaid. Higher PCL usually signals management expects more borrowers to default.
Net Interest Margin
The difference between what a bank earns on loans and what it pays on deposits. A key measure of bank profitability.
Overnight Rate
The interest rate at which major financial institutions borrow and lend one-day funds. The Bank of Canada's primary policy tool.
Yield Curve
A graph showing interest rates on bonds across different maturities. Typically slopes upward; inversion often precedes recession.
CET1 Ratio
Common Equity Tier 1 — a measure of a bank's core capital relative to its risk-weighted assets. Higher ratio = more financially stable bank.
Duration
A measure of a bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes. Longer duration means greater price impact from rate moves in either direction.
Suitability
The regulatory obligation to ensure investment recommendations are appropriate for a client's financial situation, risk tolerance, and objectives.
Basis Point
One hundredth of one percentage point (0.01%). Used to describe small changes in interest rates or spreads. 25bps = 0.25%.
PCL (Performing)
Provisions against loans currently performing but statistically expected to generate future losses based on economic models.
